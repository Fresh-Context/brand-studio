Why this matters
We sell a claim: that we help organizations put AI to work without losing what makes them themselves. Our own brand is the first proof of that claim - every page, proposal, post, and image a prospect sees is either building one clear impression of Fresh Context or muddying it.

For a firm this small, consistency is leverage; it makes a team of two read like a firm with a real point of view. And it is fragile: the more people and AI we put to work making collateral, the more ways the brand drifts. Drift is quiet - no single slip looks like a problem; the accumulation is what costs us credibility. The goal is simple to say and hard to do: everything we put in front of a customer should look and sound like the same firm, whoever or whatever made it.
What "on-brand" looks like
Voice - operator-first, prescriptive, specific, honest about what is broken; not a consulting deck.
Language - our own words used the same way every time, never watered down.
Look - hand-drawn scientific illustration: brown ink on cream, one orange accent. No stock, no generic vector, no glassy AI look.
Fit - the format matches where the reader is; a thesis, a case study, and a proposal are different jobs.

When it works, you cannot tell whether Sam, a teammate, or an AI drafted something. When it does not, you get the tells: em dashes that read as AI-written, our terms diluted, a visual that could belong to anyone.
How we approach it with AI
AI is the reason we can make far more collateral, far faster. It is also why the brand can drift faster, because out of the box AI produces competent, average, off-brand work. The obvious fix - paste the guidelines into every prompt - does not hold: nobody does it every time, it comes out different each time, and it quietly rots.

So we do the same thing we tell clients: treat the brand as shared context every AI session already has. We write it down once, and it comes along automatically - for every person, in every session - instead of being remembered, or forgotten, each time. The payoff is the whole point: more customer-facing work, faster, on-brand by default. This is us running our own thesis on ourselves.
One brand, three ways it reaches the work
We write the brand down in one place. /brand-sync publishes it, with a review first. From then on it reaches the work three ways - always on, on demand, and as a quality check - so being on-brand is the default, not an extra step anyone has to remember.

The always-on rules reach everyone today, with nothing to run. The on-demand side - deeper guidance and the image library - runs on Sam's machine for now; deploying it is the next step, and it will move this page to studio.freshcontext.ai. If Studio is down, a session still has the core brand; it just can't pull the deeper library or generate images. You're reading this from the Studio app, so it's live right now.
Under the hood - the technical implementation
The decision underneath all of this is authoring versus serving, not belief versus rules. One home authors the brand - our knowledge vault, where belief and the operational tables live together. Studio publishes and serves a read-only cache in which every served rule carries provenance back to its source note. Nothing is authoritative in two places. It is our own product theses applied to our own brand: "one architecture, many systems"; "publication is a process, not a status"; "two doors, one API" - these human screens and our AI sessions hit the same endpoints.

How we made the call
We considered and rejected: a session-start hook (it does not reach sub-agents, and adds an "is it installed" failure mode), a plugin (git already distributes a committed file to everyone), and making the always-on rules a pull-on-demand skill (voice cannot wait to be fetched). A generated, committed file gives us freshness with none of the machinery.
Provenance and honest findings
Every served rule carries its source note and compile date - see the live strip at the top (currently 22 rules, compiled 2026-07-12, each traceable to the vault note it came from). Open findings from the review that built this page: the on-demand side needs a running service that is not yet always-on, and our knowledge-base index is currently laptop-bound; one deployment fixes both. The always-on lane is unaffected - it is a committed file that reaches everyone regardless.
